# Humanizer — training record

Generated by `data/scripts/make_report.py` from the run artefacts. Do not edit by hand.

## Corpus

Human documents are published papers whose text predates the public release of ChatGPT
(2022-11-30), so the prose cannot contain generated text. Machine documents are LLM-written
passages on the same titles, produced without sight of the human text.

### English

- 100 papers, venue confirmed for every one (24 from arXiv `journal_ref`, 76 from an acceptance statement in the author comment).
- Venue families: CV 13, HCI 13, IR_DM 13, ML 13, NLP 12, ROB_GRAPH 12, SEC 12, SYS_SE 12.
- Downloaded 139 candidates, 100 survived body extraction and selection.
- Text: `pdftotext -enc UTF-8`, body isolated between the Introduction heading and the
  bibliography, captions / page furniture / equation dumps removed.

### Chinese

- 100 papers: 53 from 软件学报 (Journal of Software, CCF-A 中文期刊), 47 from 计算机系统应用 (Computer Systems & Applications).
- Issues restricted to 2019-2021 (pre-ChatGPT).
- Sources limited to journals that publish full text openly. CNKI and Wanfang were not
  touched: their full text is licensed and scraping it would breach that licence.
- Text: PyMuPDF with sorted reading order. Chinese journals set Chinese sentences with
  ASCII periods and commas; those are mapped back to full-width forms before segmentation,
  without which the sentence splitter saw one 700-character sentence per paragraph.

### Controlled confounds

Four measurements separate the two corpora almost perfectly and are deliberately **excluded**
from the feature set, because they separate them for a reason that does not hold on a real
AI-drafted manuscript:

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| citation markers | a synthetic passage has no bibliography (Cohen's d was -1.8) |
| parentheses | dominated by glosses, cross-references and `(N=24)` (d was -2.0) |
| quotation marks | a synthetic passage quotes no participants |
| acronyms / proper nouns | a synthetic passage invents generic system and dataset names |

Citation markers are also stripped from **both** sides before any feature is computed.
A model trained on these would learn *has a bibliography, therefore human*.

Two machine variants were generated per paper. v1 was produced under a prompt that told the
model not to hedge, which suppressed hedge density on the machine side and inverted the sign
of a feature that matters; v2 uses a neutral prompt. Both are kept — they are two registers an
LLM actually produces — and the machine class is down-weighted to parity with the human class.

## Model

Gradient-boosted decision stumps under logistic loss, implemented in numpy. Depth-1 trees make
the model a generalised additive model: the total contribution of each feature to a score is
exactly computable, which is what lets a flagged passage come with a reason.

Splitting is at the **paper** level; a machine document inherits the split of the human paper
it was matched to. Window-level splitting would leak, because two windows from one paper share
topic, vocabulary and author.

## Limits

- The Chinese band is a **computer-science** register from two journals, not Chinese academic
  writing in general.
- The English side is the arXiv rendering of published papers, not the publisher's typeset copy.
- The machine class comes from one model family at one point in time. It will drift. `features.py`
  carries a version that the scorer checks at load time and refuses to score on mismatch.
- `rare_token_share` is measured against a 4000-token common core fit on the training papers.
  A manuscript from a distant subfield will sit high on it for reasons that are not register.
- Nothing here establishes authorship. See `references/what-the-score-means.md`.
